So back in 11 May, I went to watch Project Hail Mary, which is perhaps one of the best movies I've watched in a long time (There isn't much competition to begin with, I don't watch much movies). It is a Sci-Fi movie that starts out mysterious and melancholic, but ends in a positive note. And to my luck, I stumbled upon the book that the movie adapted from! So during my commuting time, I read the book.

Obviously, the story in the book and the movie are the same, perhaps only some scene exclusion or changes. Therefore, there isn't need for me to reexplain everything. If you don't know what happens in Project Hail Mary, please go watch it first.

Cut scenes

The major difference in the book is that there is less detail during the time Grace was on Earth, especailly during the work in Project Hail Mary. There was more scenes with Dubois and Sharpio, the scientists planned to be on the Hail Mary (but sadly died before that), and several other people who worked on parts of the Hail Mary (like the robot arm, the Beetles, the design of the ship. One part I love is when Lokken, who came point out a design flaw in Hail Mary, upon seeing Grace, the two immediately started arguing due to Grace's infamous paper and stance on alien lifeforms. But when they started discussing the gravity issue in the ship, they locked in and gave insights to fix the problem. And in the end, Stratt uses her power to forcibly hire Lokken into the team, which left Lokken dumbfounded, and Grace was like "You'll get used to it".

The movie also cut the scene where Antarctica got nuked, which is pretty controversial to the fans. In the book, because of the astrophage infection, combined with placing massive astrophage farms in the Sahara desert (which is also mentioned in the book), the ecosystem was severely disturbed. Lecleric, a climatologist who deeply cares about the environment, pointed out that humanity would only have nineteen years left to spare, which is not enough for the mission. In order to buy some more time, the solution they ended up with is to nuke Antarctica and release the trapped greenhouse gas. Lecleric spent his life studying and advocating to protect the ecosystem, but now he is the one to destroy it in order to give Earth more time. It was incredibly heartbreaking, and also shows the extend Stratt would go to save humanity, even if it's immoral.

On the other hand, the movie added was the karaoke scene, where Stratt sang for the departing crew during the farewell party. Some people complained the mpvie should have added the scene in Antarctica instead, but I don't know if I should agree. While that scene is very impactful, I really loved the softer side we see of Stratt. She spent most of the story being cold due to the project, she couldn't let emotions get hold of important decisions. But in the karaoke scene, she decided to let her guard down for a short moment, and share her feelings with the crew. I actually really loved that moment, I believe both scenes add to Stratt's character, just in different ways.

Scientific details vs Emotional effects

Another special thing about the book is how much it focuses on the experiements. In the movie, there isn't much focus on how the characters figure out things, like how Grace figured out he is in space or he wasn't near the Solar system at all, it is a split moment thing. But in the book, he had to figure it all out using experiements, like dropping a test tube on the floor and calculating the fall time to check if the gravity is heavier, or checking the position of the sun spot to see whether the sun is our sun. I really love the focus on the methodologies in the book.

For the movie, it focuses on the emotional aspect more. Of course, it is easier to show the emotional parts in movies, it has moving things, special effects and music, while books only have paper and text. When I was reading the scenes I found particularly emotional in the movie, it felt weaker in the book, which makes sense if you compare it to the movie. The movie is really great in terms of music and emotional composition.

While the book is really good (I managed to finish it without feeling lightheaded or disoriented about what is going on), I still prefer the movie a lot. There is many other differences I haven't pointed out, but yea I think it's a great experience reading both versions.
