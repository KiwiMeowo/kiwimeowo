Calling All Villains

I am not very caught up in the current fandom stuff, but I heard this show has been rising in popularity! I didn't immediately watch it, because something is popular, doesn't mean I should watch it. But my sister played it on TV one day, so I watched it... It has a Battle Royale style game here! I'm all about these type of stories and nobody told me about it? But technically nobody got eliminated yet, so who knows.

This is a show about Mr Puzzles, who is losing his direction in life, decided to join a Battle-Royale style competition to be the new great villain after the Big Bad... If a member gets eliminated, they are (probably) killed. Nobody got killed from being eliminated yet, except for Dave but he died from trying to attack the Big Bad so I don't count that. There's so many charming characters, I especially like the Pink Thing, I love her four ears, and her name is very cute (Also her voice actor voiced Cassidy from Project Eden's Garden! It makes sense why her voice feels familiar now). But being in this game, the chances are that our favourite characters will die one by one, and the fact Mr Puzzle's friend, WPNZ, is also there, it won't end well.

In the first round, Mr Puzzle's team has to go to the Paper Planet and steal the king's pencil, which can grant anything the user writes down. Of course, being a team of wannabe villains, they immediately started in-fighting.

I really found the part funny when the villains accidentally broke the pencil tip, Mr Puzzles just said the pencil had a baby and started roleplaying as the baby pencil lead. The voice acting just makes me laugh.

Well luckily they eventually did stop, Mr Puzzles pretended to be a jester to enter the castle, and the others helped distracting the guards. When Mr Puzzles tried to steal the King's pencil, the king started crying and venting about how he couldn't be as great as his father. With the king's vulnerability, Mr Puzzles also shared a piece of his past. So the king suggested to run away together, and proceeded to swallow the pencil. Mr Puzzles, feeling desperate, smacks the King with a staff. Even after realising the jester is actually a villain, the king still viewed him as an ally. Mr Puzzles put down the staff, but did something else: Using the broken off pencil lead to write down "The King Shatters". Mission complete, and luckily they passed the test.

People seemed to be quite devastated by this scene, but when it went down, I was like "Kill him kill him kill him" the entire time, chat am I evil?

I remember someone saying that when Mr Puzzles killed the king, he used the most indirect way possible. He could've just smashed the king with the staff, or write "The King Dies" or something, but writing the king shatters seems to distance him from the king's death, even if he's the one who's responsible for all of it. I feel like it's an important characterization of him.

There's also a lot of theories going on, like Lady Killer might be an undercover hero, or the paper king will be a parallel to the Big Bad... Maybe I can search for more, but it feels like the theories feels so feasible, it felt like spoilers.

One thing I haven't mentioned is that Mr Puzzles and WPNZ aren't new characters, they are originally from the SMG4 series, which I only know about the unusual animation style. I don't think you need to watch SMG4 to understand CAV, but it does help understanding the two characters a bit more. Mr Puzzles was very evil back in the day, but he decided to change. I'd thought he would be less guilty about being evil considering his history, but in fact he seems to be unhappy about the possibility of betraying his friends again.

My thoughts is that the animation is very splendid, while many indie shows I've watched have really nice animations and styles, but watching CAV felt like watching a movie. The story seems promising, but I'm not ready to say if it's a show I love. It's still a pilot, so I'll wait!

Polly Polly Possum

This show is a bit more on the meta aspect. This is a show about a comic series being adapted into a more modern show, but Melissa, Polly's (the show's protagonist) neighbour, was having flashbacks to her former life as the main villain, Miss Malice. Being confused about all the memories, she's now conflicted if she should continue her past life.

The episodes for this series is a lot shorter, less then five minutes. I really found the theme very unique, because what's more fun than watching fictional characters getiing more awareness? It also focuses on the concept how stories and characters may change during adaptations, and makes it into the character's struggles and motivations. Something I overlooked a little bit is the newspaper content during the credits, it contains some articles about the fans, the author and some regular stuff, but it might be relevant to the characters stories. One person pointed out that the reason the other characters viewed Polly as erratic might be because Polly's action is tied to fan polls, where they can decide who Polly can date with.

Another theory I really liked is one of the characters, Mr Loops, is aware that he's in a story. While the other characters only knows there's another world at most, Mr Loops referred Polly as the "Main Girl", which might have hinted that Mr Loops knows that Polly is a protagonist.

Meta stuff is my cup of tea honestly, while the videos are short, the story is very interesting.

Life updates

Fingertip status

If you don't know, my fingertips tend to peel off every winter. I think last year my fingertips were fine, but it's usually a problem, and it's not like there's anything to prevent or help with the situation, hand creams don't work.

As winter approached this year, I noticed my right thumb skin has gone thicker, and it kept snatching onto fabric. I was kinda confused, since I never encountered it before. So I just kept peeling at it, or cutting of any bits coming off so that it won't get stuck or make me feel uncomfortable.

Last night when I was showering, I took a look at my fingers, and finally remembered why. For the past month, I have been making plushies, and I make their faces by embroidering. Pushing needles into the fabric can be hard work, sometimes the embroidered area becomes so thick, it's difficult to get the needle through, and it might hurt my fingers (And I would think to myself, man I need a thimble... Wait why do I know that word-).

So the reason why my thumb tip skin got thickened is callus forming while embroidering, and when I was showering, the contrast between the normal skin and calloused skin is clearer. And it's not only my thumb that has the callus, my right index finger also has it! So yea, luckily my winter fingertip thing didn't evolve and stuff.
