Rant on art protection tools against AI

The pains of sharing art online

Weather: Cold

Listening to: Kyou No Hi Wa Sayounara (Chorus Version)

Mood: Eepy

Random thought: Did absolutely nothing today

Tags: Discussion, Art
882 words (3 min read)

Being an artist, I too, have concerns of someone taking my art and use it to train AI or process my images with AI. I have done a few things to prevent that from happening (or at least, discourage malicious people): Posting less art on social media, adding robot.txt to my site, posting the low resolution versions of my pics. There's also some tools that people would recommend, which claimed to prevent AI from replicating your artstyle, or even poison the AI models if enough poisoned images got into its database. However, I often feel sceptical about them, and it's not (just) because they might not work.

First thing out of the way, my opinion on AI art, or any AI-generated content, has gotten more and more negative over the years. Like there's also one blog where I talked about AI chatbots (which I have removed I think), but now I just don't want to touch them. While there's a lot of reasons to dislike genAI, one thing that gets in my nerves the most is the AI bros who specifically trolls artists by stealing their art and put them through AI models.

In order to avoid these kind of trolls, some people would figure out ways to protect their art, maybe use tools like Glaze, Nightshade, ArtShield. However, some people (especially pro Ai people) would make fun of artists trying out these tools, because they claimed the tools are already bypassed, the tools are just snake oil, and say "their art looks too ugly to be scraped", which totally doesn't sound like those victim-blaming people. I always take these claims with a grain of salt, because just maybeeee the tools do work and they are lying to discourage us.

But after a bit of digging, I have found other problems about these tools, and I don't think enough people really pointed out.

High spec requirements

As a Mac user, I am very unfortunate that I often encounter apps that can't be used in my computer. Glaze and Nightshade are very popular tools advertised by artists, but I can't use Nightshade because it requires Apple CPUs, which my Mac uses Intel. And as for Glaze, while theoretically it can open in my Mac. When I did download it, it just refused to launch. It is said that both of these applications require high-end computers, so that sucks.

(There's also WebGlaze, which is the website alternative, but I've heard of people who waited for a long time and still haven't received an invite code)

Lack of updates

These tools started coming up during 2023-2024, which is almost 3 years ago. It seems like a short amount of time, but for technological advancements, and especially for AI, it has already evolved by a lot. But for these protection tools, the updates (that aren't bug fixes) are very long ago:

  • Glaze: 2024 Apr 14
  • NightShade: 2024 Jan 18 (It is the launch release date)
  • ArtShield: 2023 Mar 7
  • Mist v2: 2024 Nov 11

Hope I didn't get anything wrong, and while I don't expect experts could update these tools all the time, I feel somewhat pessimistic about how reliable these tools are.

How do they even work

I've actually tried to use Mist v2 before, I used Google Colab to run it... And I kept running into errors. Like damn do I not have any devices that can properly run these? It might be skill issue, but I assume those who are less familiar with tech would have a even harder time figuring it out.

Weak to compression

Among all the tools I've found, the most user-friendly and accessible tools is ArtShield. But after putting it through the tool, the image size becomes really massive: The maximum size I'd accept is like 1MB ish, but the processed images are like 10MB. And according to ArtShield: "ArtShield is susceptible to cropping, rotation, and resizing.", so putting them through compression tools would make it useless. In fact, if you put images to social media (which is the place artists worry about scraping the most), the images get compressed anyway!

Probably unreliable tool?

There IS one that is released in 2026, which is FoxGlove- Actually nevermind, recently it updated and removed the poison feature because it doesn't work properly apparently. But I was already suspicious of it before the update, because the code is all in the <script>, which I would assume anyone could just find a way to reverse the poisoning.

While I pointed out the many reasons why these tools are inaccessible or ineffective at times, I don't mean it as a diss towards the developers, and I know keeping up it very difficult, especially our current situation. Aside from wanting to vent out some frustrations, I also want developers of these tools to consider the target audience, who might not have high-end computers and might not be very knowledgeable about these technology (Many of them aren't very aware that Glaze isn't effective towards img2img, which means Grok isn't stopped at all).

While I don't know if there is a truely effective way to protect my art, but I really wish the solution isn't just "not posting anything online". One of my joy of making art is that I can tell it to other like-minded people.
